The capacitance of a cube

22 November 2026 · Hrvoje Abraham, Fable 5.2

A metal sphere of radius rrr has the capacitance 4πε0r4\pi\varepsilon_0 r4πε0​r. For a metal cube no formula is known, only numbers. This post has two results.

The first is a series of positive terms for that number, made of charged spherical shells. Each term is an algebraic number from a closed formula, with no integral. The series is exact, but too slow to give many decimals.

The second is a fast series of the same structure, made of charged patches on the faces. It gives

Ccube=4πε0 s κ,κ=0.660 678 154 099 568 000±10−18 \small C_{\text{cube}} = 4\pi\varepsilon_0\, s\,\kappa, \qquad \kappa = 0.660\,678\,154\,099\,568\,000 \pm 10^{-18} Ccube​=4πε0​sκ,κ=0.660678154099568000±10−18

for a cube of side sss. The error is an estimate from the convergence, not a proved bound.

The problem

Put the unit cube KKK at potential 1, in units where the potential of a point charge qqq at distance rrr is q/rq/rq/r. The charge moves to the surface and settles in an equilibrium distribution μ\muμ. Its total is the number κ\kappaκ. In these units a sphere of radius rrr holds the charge rrr, so κ\kappaκ is the radius of the sphere that is equivalent to the cube.

The density of μ\muμ is not known. It is infinite along the edges and still stronger at the corners, and this is the full difficulty of the problem.

One classical fact makes the problem approachable without the density. Take any charge distribution ν\nuν in the cube, not necessarily in equilibrium, with total charge q(ν)q(\nu)q(ν) and energy

E(ν)=∬dν(x) dν(y)∣x−y∣. E(\nu) = \iint \frac{d\nu(x)\, d\nu(y)}{|x-y|}. E(ν)=∬∣x−y∣dν(x)dν(y)​.

Then

2 q(ν)−E(ν)  =  κ−E(μ−ν)  ≤  κ. 2\,q(\nu) - E(\nu) \;=\; \kappa - E(\mu - \nu) \;\le\; \kappa . 2q(ν)−E(ν)=κ−E(μ−ν)≤κ.

The proof is one line. The potential of μ\muμ is 1 on the whole cube, thus the mutual energy of μ\muμ and ν\nuν is q(ν)q(\nu)q(ν), and E(μ−ν)=κ−2q(ν)+E(ν)E(\mu-\nu) = \kappa - 2q(\nu) + E(\nu)E(μ−ν)=κ−2q(ν)+E(ν) cannot be negative. Each guess ν\nuν gives a lower bound for κ\kappaκ, and the error of the bound is the energy of the error of the guess. A guess with an error of 10−810^{-8}10−8 gives κ\kappaκ to 10−1610^{-16}10−16.

Now take NNN fixed distributions σ1,…,σN\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_Nσ1​,…,σN​ of unit charge and let the guess be ν=∑qjσj\nu = \sum q_j \sigma_jν=∑qj​σj​. The best charges qjq_jqj​ follow from a linear system, and the bound becomes

κN=1TG−1 1  ≤  κ,Gij=E(σi,σj). \kappa_N = \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T} G^{-1}\, \mathbf 1 \;\le\; \kappa, \qquad G_{ij} = E(\sigma_i, \sigma_j). κN​=1TG−11≤κ,Gij​=E(σi​,σj​).

The system has a physical meaning. The product (Gq)i(Gq)_i(Gq)i​ is the potential of all the trial charges, averaged over σi\sigma_iσi​. Thus Gq=1Gq = \mathbf 1Gq=1 says: the potential is 1 on each σi\sigma_iσi​ on average. This is the conductor condition, as far as the family can test it. Why it is slow

The reason is the edges. Near an edge the density grows like d−1/3d^{-1/3}d−1/3, where ddd is the distance to the edge, and at a corner it grows faster, with an irrational power. Each of these adds its own sequence of fractional powers of 1/n1/n1/n to the error. A sphere has the same size in all directions. To follow the density at distance ddd from an edge, the shells there must have a radius near ddd, and then 1/d1/d1/d of them are necessary along the edge. No arrangement of shells escapes this count.

Patches

The cure is to let the trial charges become thin. Put the charge on the faces, on rectangular patches that are a fixed fraction of the neighbour patch in the direction of each edge. Twenty-three layers reach from the centre of a face to 10−1310^{-13}10−13 from the edge. On each patch the density is a polynomial, of a higher degree on the large patches in the middle and of a low degree on the small ones near the edges.

The energies of two patches are fourfold integrals of 1/r1/r1/r, and they must be accurate to all digits. One identity makes them easy:

1r=2π∫0∞e−t2r2 dt  ≈  ∑kwk e−tk2r2. \frac1r = \frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-t^2 r^2}\,dt \;\approx\; \sum_k w_k\, e^{-t_k^2 r^2}. r1​=π​2​∫0∞​e−t2r2dt≈k∑​wk​e−tk2​r2.

With t=eut = e^ut=eu the integrand is smooth and decreases fast in both directions, and the plain trapezoidal rule with a step hhh in uuu is enough. Its relative error is the same for all distances rrr. The Fourier transform of the integrand is a gamma function, and this gives the bound 22 e−π2/2h2\sqrt2\, e^{-\pi^2/2h}22​e−π2/2h. I picked h=0.12h = 0.12h=0.12 as the largest round step with a bound below the target: 4⋅10−184\cdot10^{-18}4⋅10−18. The work grows like 1/h1/h1/h, so a smaller step only costs time. A Gaussian of rrr is a product of Gaussians of the coordinates. Thus each energy is a sum of products of integrals in one variable,

Integration by parts moves all derivatives to the polynomials, and what stays are repeated integrals of the Gaussian at the differences of the interval ends. These are combinations of erf⁡\operatorname{erf}erf and exp⁡\expexp. Again there is no numerical integration over the cube.

How the system is solved

Why HC=mHC = mHC=m? It is the same statement as Gq=1Gq = \mathbf 1Gq=1 for the shells. Add a small charge δ\deltaδ of the shape φaφb\varphi_a\varphi_bφa​φb​ to the faces. The bound 2q−E2q - E2q−E changes in proportion to δ (mab−(HC)ab)\delta\,(m_{ab} - (HC)_{ab})δ(mab​−(HC)ab​), and at the best CCC no such change can help, so all these differences are zero.

Physically, (HC)ab(HC)_{ab}(HC)ab​ is the potential of the full trial charge on a face, weighted with φaφb\varphi_a\varphi_bφa​φb​ and integrated, and mabm_{ab}mab​ is the same integral of the potential 1. So the system says: the surface is at potential 1, as far as the trial functions can test it. For the functions of degree 0 this means that the mean potential over each patch is exactly 1. The higher degrees also remove its mean slope and curvature over the patch. A conductor has the potential 1 at every point of its surface. The system asks for this in as many ways as there are unknowns. The matrix HHH is symmetric and positive definite, and it is full: each patch acts on each other patch. The density must not change when xxx and yyy change places, so Cab=CbaC_{ab} = C_{ba}Cab​=Cba​, and NNN functions on the edge give N(N+1)/2N(N+1)/2N(N+1)/2 unknowns. The largest run has N=343N = 343N=343 and 58 996 unknowns.

The solution has three stages.

  1. Scale HHH to a unit diagonal, because the energies of the small patches near the edges are much larger than the energies of the large ones.
  2. Factor H=LLTH = LL^{\mathsf T}H=LLT with Cholesky in double precision, and solve Lz=mLz = mLz=m. Then

The programs

Both programs are plain Julia with threads, without packages:

  • shells.jl makes the two shell tables. The matrix is assembled over the orbits of the 48 symmetries. The values QnQ_nQn​ are refined to 28 digits with double-double residuals, so that the extrapolation does not amplify rounding errors.
  • patches.jl makes the patch table. The integrals in one variable cancel strongly and use 1024-bit numbers. The matrix HHH and its Cholesky factor are in double precision, and the solution is refined with double-double residuals.
julia -t auto shells.jl 512
julia -t auto patches.jl

On a 32-thread desktop (Core i9-13900K, 64 GB) the shell tables take about 2 minutes, most of it for n=512n = 512n=512, and the six default rows of the patch table take 8 minutes. The last row of the patch table is julia -t auto patches.jl 32 14 0.75. It needs 19 minutes and about 30 GB.

Notes

Is this new? The idea is not. It is the Rayleigh–Ritz method: restrict a variational problem to a finite family and solve a linear system. Each part has a name and a history.

  • The energy principle is due to Gauss and Thomson. For the capacity it is the base of Pólya and Szegő, Isoperimetric Inequalities in Mathematical Physics (1951).
  • A finite family with the matrix of energies is Maxwell’s method of subareas, in electrical engineering the method of moments (Harrington, 1968), and with the energy as the scalar product the Galerkin boundary element method. The formula 1TG−11\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T} G^{-1} \mathbf 11TG−11 with its error identity is in Betcke, Haberl and Praetorius, Adaptive boundary element methods for the computation of the electrostatic capacity on complex polyhedra (2019).
  • The energy of two overlapping shells is, up to a factor, the trace of the Rotne–Prager–Yamakawa tensor of hydrodynamics (Zuk, Wajnryb, Mizerski and Szymczak, 2014). There the same sum over the inverse matrix gives the hydrodynamic radius of a chain of beads.
  • G=LLTG = LL^{\mathsf T}G=LLT and ∑zj2\sum z_j^2∑zj2​ are Cholesky, Gram–Schmidt and Parseval.
  • Patches graded in a fixed ratio toward the edges, with polynomials on them, are the hp boundary element method, which is known to converge exponentially for such densities.
  • The sum of Gaussians for 1/r1/r1/r is a standard tool as well: sinc quadrature (Stenger), and Beylkin and Monzón, Approximation by exponential sums revisited (2010).

What can be particular to this post is the combination: the nested shell family in this order as an explicit series, and the patch system with all integrals reduced to erf⁡\operatorname{erf}erf. I did not find the shell series in the literature, and a short search showed no published value of κ\kappaκ with more than 14 digits. Neither search was complete, and I do not claim that anything here is new.

The lower bounds are exact statements about exact arithmetic. That the bounds converge to κ\kappaκ, and how many digits are final, rests here on the numbers: on the agreement of runs with different meshes, degrees and parameters, and on the agreement with the 13 digits of Helsing and Perfekt, On the polarizability and capacitance of the cube (2013), 0.660678154099570.660678154099570.66067815409957.
